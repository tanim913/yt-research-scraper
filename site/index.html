Spend quota on comments, not on searching

YouTube gives each key 10,000 units a day. A search costs 100 of them, so one key is gone after about 100 searches. Listing a channel’s uploads costs about 1 unit per 25 videos, and rules are applied on your computer, for free.

Costs from YouTube’s Data API v3. A search returns at most about 500 results.

Scraping comments costs 1 unit per 80–100 comments. About 86,000 comments from three news channels over two and a half years is roughly 1,000–1,500 units, which fits in one key’s day.

Your rules are your methodology

Every rule is explicit, saved as a preset, and easy to write up in a methods section. The guide has a template you can copy.

  • KeywordsInclude any or all, and exclude, in title, description and tags
  • EngagementMinimum and maximum views, likes, comments and length
  • DiscussionComments per 1,000 views, to find videos people argue about
  • DatesPublished from and until
  • LanguageDrops videos whose uploader set a different language
  • CategoryNews, education, and the rest of YouTube’s categories
  • SkipShorts, live streams, made-for-kids videos, comments turned off
  • ReviewCross out anything the rules can’t catch

When the quota runs out, it waits

Add keys from different Google Cloud projects and they add up. Progress is saved after every page of results, so a crash, a closed laptop or an empty quota loses nothing.

  • Key 1 runs dry

    It moves to the key with the most quota left.

  • Key 2 runs dry

    And on to the next one, automatically.

  • All keys used

    The job pauses. Your data is safe on disk.

  • It carries on

    After the reset at midnight Pacific time, or the moment you add a key.

One tidy CSV, ready for Python or Excel

Comment text is saved exactly as written, in UTF-8 with a BOM, so Bangla, Russian and other scripts show correctly in Excel. Replies point to their parent comment.

Windows says “protected your PC”Press More info, then Run anyway. The apps are not code-signed.

Mac says “unidentified developer”Right-click the file and press Open. If it won’t run, open Terminal and run chmod +x on the file first.

Keep its window openClosing it stops the app. Your keys and data are saved in .yt-research-scraper in your home folder.
